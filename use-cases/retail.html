The Problem

The decision most teams are making

Most site selection relies on what's visible. What determines performance is almost never visible.

Signal 01

Foot traffic counts

Aggregate pedestrian counts tell you how many people pass — not whether they match your concept's income profile, purchase frequency, or category behavior.

Signal 02

Nearby brand presence

Seeing recognized brands in a corridor feels like validation. It isn't. It tells you nothing about saturation levels, opening dates, or whether those concepts are performing.

Signal 03

Broker input

Brokers know what's available and what's leased. They rarely have access to the data needed to assess whether a location structurally supports your concept at your target unit economics.

The factors that actually determine whether a retail location performs — competitor saturation, mobility patterns by time and mode, demographic alignment at the submarket level, zoning constraints — are rarely visible from street level or a site tour.

What BlackPrint Shows

The variables that actually determine site performance

Four layers of structured intelligence, available for any trade area in Latin America.

Competitive Intelligence

Real competitive density

Full mapping of competitor brands, categories, and opening dates within any trade area. Not just what's visible — what's operating, when it opened, and how saturated the corridor already is at the category level.

Know exactly what you're entering before you negotiate a lease.

Demographics

Trade area demographics

Income segmentation, population density, and daytime versus nighttime population — structured to match against your concept's customer profile. See whether the people in a trade area are actually the people your format serves.

Validate demographic fit before committing capital.

Mobility Patterns

Traffic patterns by time and mode

Pedestrian and vehicle intensity by hour and day of week. Understand whether a location performs on weekdays, weekends, or both — and whether the traffic is on foot, by car, or transit-dependent.

Match location cadence to your store's operating model.

The Workflow Change

From instinct-driven to evidence-driven site evaluation

Before

Field visits and broker tours

Teams spend weeks visiting candidate locations, relying on observation and broker commentary to form a view. Competitive and demographic context is assembled manually — if at all.

Before

Disconnected data sources

Traffic studies, demographic reports, and competitive maps live in separate systems, different formats, and often different timeframes. Synthesis is manual, slow, and prone to gaps.

Before

Hard to defend internally

Site recommendations are built on judgment. When leadership asks for the rationale, the answer is often a combination of comparable sites, broker confidence, and foot traffic counts — none of which are structurally rigorous.

Define trade area → Layer competitive density → Validate demographics → Confirm mobility fit → Surface available sites

One session. One system. A defensible shortlist before any field time is spent.

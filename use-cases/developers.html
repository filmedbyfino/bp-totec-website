The Problem

Development decisions are made years before the market responds

By the time a project delivers, the demand environment has shifted — often in ways that were visible in the data at acquisition, but never surfaced in the analysis.

Gap 01

Retail mix chosen by convention

Which categories anchor a mixed-use development is often determined by what's worked before, what brokers recommend, and what's available in the tenant pipeline — not by what the specific trade area's population actually demands.

Gap 02

Zoning context assembled manually

Understanding what can be built — permitted uses, height and FAR restrictions, pending permits nearby — requires coordinating between municipal sources, legal reviews, and field research. The process is slow and the data is rarely current.

Gap 03

Competitive supply gaps invisible

Identifying corridors where population demand exists but supply is structurally thin requires category-level competitive mapping at submarket resolution. That data doesn't exist in most feasibility studies.

Development decisions grounded in market reality — not broker intuition or the categories that anchor other projects — require a different kind of data infrastructure. One that connects what the population demands with what the competitive environment already supplies.

What BlackPrint Shows

The market intelligence layer your feasibility study is missing

Three structured data layers that connect land use context, population demand, and competitive supply in a single system.

Land Use

Zoning and land use context

Permitted uses, height restrictions, floor area ratio limits, and pending permit activity in the surrounding area — structured and current. Understand the regulatory envelope your project sits within before committing to a program, and identify zoning changes in adjacent parcels that may affect your project's competitive position after delivery.

Ground your program in regulatory reality from day one.

Demand Analysis

Demand signals by category

Which retail categories are underserved in your target corridor — based on the population profile and competitive supply already in the market. See where demand exists for food and beverage, convenience, fitness, services, and specialty retail that isn't being met by current supply. Match your tenant mix to structural demand, not project precedent.

Build a retail mix that tenants will compete to occupy and customers will actually use.

Supply Analysis

Competitive supply gaps

Where demand exists but supply is thin — identifying opportunity corridors at the category and submarket level. Map which categories are oversupplied, which are undersupplied, and where the gap is large enough to support a new anchored concept. See opening dates and operational status of existing supply to distinguish established competition from at-risk inventory.

Enter corridors where the market will absorb your project, not resist it.

Application

Where this intelligence changes the decision

Acquisition

Before you buy the land

Validate that the corridor's population profile and competitive supply environment can absorb the development program you're underwriting. Identify which retail categories will anchor the project versus which will struggle to lease. Understand zoning constraints that affect buildable area before legal due diligence begins.

Program Design

Before you finalize the program

Design the retail mix against actual demand signals — income segmentation, category underservement, daytime versus nighttime population ratios. Avoid building formats the market won't absorb at the rents you need to underwrite, and identify categories where demand will support premium tenants.

Leasing

When you're leasing up the project

Support tenant conversations with structured market data — trade area demographics, traffic patterns, competitive set analysis — that positions your project credibly against alternatives in their expansion pipeline. Give prospective tenants a reason to commit faster.

Capital

When you're presenting to capital

Replace market narrative with structured evidence. Show investors the demand signal that justifies the project, the competitive supply gap that creates the opportunity, and the population profile that supports the underwritten rents — all from a single analytical system.

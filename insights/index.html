Why Mexico's Tier-2 Cities Are Outpacing CDMX for Retail Expansion

Querétaro, León, and San Luis Potosí are absorbing expansion mandates that would have gone to CDMX five years ago. The structural shift is driven by three factors: industrial migration pulling workforce populations, lower occupancy costs, and a competitive density gap that CDMX submarkets no longer offer. For expansion teams evaluating where to allocate next, this matters.

The Cannibalization Problem No One Models Until It's Too Late

Most brands treat cannibalization as a risk to acknowledge, not a variable to model. The result is a pattern we see repeatedly: stores opened too close, network performance diluted, and a post-mortem analysis that could have been a pre-decision model. The data to prevent this exists. The question is whether it's structured before the decision, or assembled after the damage.

What "Foot Traffic Data" Actually Means — and Why Most Teams Are Using It Wrong

Foot traffic counts measure who passes by. Trade area analysis measures who could be a customer. These are different questions, and the confusion between them produces systematically bad site selection. Raw mobility data without demographic overlay and temporal segmentation tells you where people are, not whether they're your customer.
